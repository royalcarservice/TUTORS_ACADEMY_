# TUTORS ACADEMY — Phase 4 · Step 6
## Scenes 5–6 — The People + The Practice

> Depends on Steps 4.1–4.5 (including the 4.4 availability amendment) and all of Phase 3.
> **Read the scene contract, the voice document, the honesty treatment, and Scenes 0–4's
> authored forms first.**
>
> **This step authors TWO scenes.** It does not touch the spine, Scenes 0–4, Scene 7–8, the
> subject system, or the primitives.
>
> **Every element in both scenes is unbuilt.** There is no live data, no tutor, no class, no
> recording, no AI, no progress. This step's central problem is honesty at scale.

---

### WHY THIS STEP EXISTS

Scene 5 answers *who teaches me?* Scene 6 answers *how does learning actually work here?*

Both currently answer **"nobody does, and it doesn't yet"** — and the naive responses are
both failures:

- **Fabricate** — invent tutors, show a mocked-up dashboard, let the visitor believe it exists.
  This is the fastest way to lose a student permanently on day two.
- **Apologise** — a page of hedges and disclaimers, which reads as a product with nothing to
  offer and wastes the visitor's time.

So this step solves that third way, and the solution is structural.

---

### THE CORE PRINCIPLE — REAL FOUNDATION, HONEST DISTANCE

> **The environments are real, complete and certified. Everything these two scenes describe
> will run *inside* an environment. So the scenes are anchored to something that already
> exists, and honest about the distance between here and there.**

- Every beat is framed as **what happens inside the environment** — which is real, built, and
  the visitor has already seen it.
- The unbuilt parts are **labelled, consistently, in the product's own design language**,
  never as a footnote.
- **Nothing simulates a working feature.** There is no fake video player, no fake dashboard,
  no fake chat, no fake tutor. **A visitor must never be able to mistake a beat for a working
  screen.**

---

### THE SIX LOCKED DECISIONS

**1. Scene 5's subject is the relationship, not a roster.**

There are no tutors. **No names, no photographs, no avatars, no bios, no credentials, no
subject lists, no availability.** Not even placeholder-shaped ones.

So Scene 5 does not present people. It presents **the relationship the product is designed
around** — anchored to what is verifiably real:

- **Tutors will own and shape an environment.** The five levers (3.1) exist: accent, atmosphere,
  motif, motion character, density. **A tutor's workspace is their environment, not a profile
  page and a video call.**
- **This is a specific, checkable design commitment** — not "expert tutors", which is
  unverifiable. **Report which parts of the scene are verifiable against the codebase and which
  are commitment.**
- **The scene must be distinguishable from every competitor's "meet our tutors" section.** If it
  could be lifted onto another tutoring site unchanged, it has failed.
- Labelled per the honesty treatment (Part 3).

**2. Scene 6 is a session's arc, not four feature blocks.**

Four beats, sequenced as **the life of one session**:

| Beat | Beat name | The arc |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Live** | You attend |
| 2 | **Recorded** | You revisit it |
| 3 | **Assisted** | You work, with the resources and the help |
| 4 | **Progress** | You see yourself move |

- **This is the `sequence` scroll behaviour's one legitimate use.** It steps through beats
  *within* a scene — exactly what `sequence` was rationed for. **State the justification.**
- **Each beat is a short statement, NOT a section.** No beat may be a feature card, and the four
  must not form a uniform grid.
- **The whole scene must be readable in under a minute.** If it takes longer, cut.
- **The beats must read as one continuous experience**, not four unrelated claims. The arc is
  the structure — a visitor should feel a session happening, not four capabilities listed.

**3. Since nothing in either scene is live, the honesty treatment IS the design.**

- **Treatment applied per element**, reusing the 3.6 convention and extending it with **at most
  three consistent status states in visitor language.** Propose the vocabulary; report it.
- **Banned devices:** dates · quarters · "coming soon" · countdowns · waitlists · email capture ·
  "notify me" · any urgency device. **Sequencing, not scheduling.**
- **The treatment is part of the scene's design language** (4.1) — it must read as *a product
  that knows exactly where it is*, not as a disclaimer, footnote, or legal notice.
- **One consolidated honest statement** at the end of Scene 6, in the 4.1 voice: **what exists
  today, and what's next, in order.** Confident, specific, no apology. **This is the page's
  single "what's live / what's next" beat.**
- **Scene 5 uses the per-element treatment only** — it does not repeat the consolidated statement.

**4. No simulated interface in any beat.**

**Banned outright, in both scenes:** video player · recording list or library · AI chat surface ·
resource browser · progress dashboard, chart, ring, or streak · class schedule or calendar ·
tutor card or profile · meeting or call UI · notification or message UI · any toolbar, panel or
chrome that reads as application furniture.

**Each beat is composed from what is real:** typography, the subject accent, motif roles
(`divider` and/or `focus` only — **never `substrate`**), and copy.

**5. Nothing is converted from these scenes.**

- **No CTA. No button. No link styled as a button. No email capture. No "notify me".**
- **The page's conversion happens at Scene 3 (the choice) and Scene 8 (the return).**
- **A roadmap beat that asks for something is selling, not telling.**
- Scene 6 may end with a **narrative line** leading into Scene 7 — **a sentence, not a control.**

**6. Copy economy is enforced.**

| Scene | Budget |
|---|---|
| Scene 5 | ≤ 2 sentences + ≤ 3 short supporting lines |
| Scene 6 | ≤ 4 beat statements (≤ 2 sentences each) + ≤ 1 consolidated statement |

**If the budget is exceeded, cut — do not extend.** Report the actual word counts.

### BUILD — PART 1: SCENE 5 — THE PEOPLE

**The job:** answer *who teaches me?* honestly, by describing a relationship rather than
inventing people.

**Composition:**
- **Restrained and human in register, not in pretence.** This is a moment of warmth on a
  technical page — achieved through **tone, pacing and typography**, not through photographs.
- **No people imagery of any kind.** No silhouette, no abstract avatar, no illustration of a
  person, no photograph. **Report the sweep.**
- **No roster, no grid, no cards.** A roster implies supply; the scene is about the relationship.
- **At most one restrained structural element** from the brand's language, or none.
- **`subjectMode: neutral`, `accentUse: none`.** No subject accent in Scene 5.

**Content — what the scene may claim:**
- **The commitment:** a tutor here owns and shapes an environment, rather than appearing in a
  listing.
- **Specific, from the real system:** the five levers — accent, atmosphere, motif, motion
  character, density. **Naming them is what makes the claim concrete and checkable**, rather
  than aspirational.
- **The distinction from the obvious alternative:** not a profile page and a video call.
- **Verifiability report required:** for each sentence, state whether it is **(a) verifiable in
  the codebase today**, **(b) a design commitment about the unbuilt tutor experience**, or
  **(c) forbidden** — and remove anything in (c).

**Mandatory deliverable — two candidates** for the scene's lead, with emphasis, risk and a
recommendation. **Report both verbatim.**

### BUILD — PART 2: SCENE 6 — THE PRACTICE

**The job:** show how learning works here, as **one session's arc**, honestly labelled.

**Composition:**
- **The beats must feel like one continuous experience.** Use the `sequence` scroll behaviour to
  step through them, **with the arc carrying the structure** — not four equal panels.
- **The four beats must not form a uniform grid.** No identical tiles, no equal-weight cards,
  no numbered feature list.
- **The scene must work as a readable story with no motion at all** — that is the test of whether
  the arc is real or whether motion was doing the work.

**Per-beat requirements:**
- **A short statement — ≤ 2 sentences.** Concrete. What happens, in the visitor's terms.
- **Grounded in the environment:** each beat happens *inside* an environment, which is real.
- **Specific over general.** *"A recording of the class you missed, at the point you stopped
  watching"* is credible. *"Seamless access to recorded sessions"* is not.
- **No simulated interface.** See Decision 4's ban list.
- **The status treatment applied per beat**, from the vocabulary proposed in Part 3.

**Mandatory deliverables:**
- **Two candidates** for Scene 6's lead, with emphasis, risk and a recommendation. Verbatim.
- **Two candidate phrasings for each beat statement** (eight total), with a recommendation per
  beat. Verbatim. **State which is more specific, and why.**
- **The consolidated honest statement** — at least **two candidates**, with a recommendation.
  Verbatim. Checked against Decision 3's bans.

### BUILD — PART 3: THE HONESTY TREATMENT, APPLIED CONSISTENTLY

**The treatment vocabulary:**
- **Propose at most three status states, in visitor language.** Extend the 3.6 convention —
  **do not invent a second convention.** State the vocabulary and its rules.
- **Apply consistently** across Scenes 5 and 6, and **report any difference from how Scenes 3
  and 4 use it** — consistency across the whole page is the goal.
- **Where the status can be derived from config, derive it** (per the 4.4 pattern) so it
  self-corrects as features ship. Report which parts are derived and which are authored.

**Consistency rules:**
- **The treatment must not read as an error state.** No red, no warning iconography, no
  exclamation.
- **It must not be smaller than the surrounding text as a way of hiding it.** It is designed
  content, not fine print.
- **It must be present in the server-rendered HTML** — not injected by JS.
- **One consolidated statement**, at the end of Scene 6, stating what exists today and what's
  next **in order**. No dates, no scheduling, no urgency.

### BUILD — PART 4: ACCESSIBILITY

- **The status is real text**, adjacent to the thing it describes, present in the DOM order a
  screen reader follows. **Not a visual badge the reader never reaches.**
- **A screen-reader user must understand the state of each beat** — which exists and which is
  coming — without seeing any styling.
- **Report the reading order** and confirm the treatment is announced sensibly, not as noise
  before or after every line.
- **The `sequence` behaviour must not trap or require scroll.** All four beats are present and
  readable with motion removed and with JS off.
- **No live-region churn** from the sequence stepping.
- **Nothing conveyed by colour alone** — including the status. Verify with a grayscale pass.
- **Heading nesting correct**; no additional `h1`.
- **Copy remains legible over any motif** — the 3.3 legibility rule holds; motifs are `divider`
  and/or `focus` only, never `substrate`.
- **Reduced motion:** the sequence collapses to a static, complete set of beats in order.
  **Screenshot it.**
- **No-JS:** all beats and statements present, in order, server-rendered. **Screenshot it.**
