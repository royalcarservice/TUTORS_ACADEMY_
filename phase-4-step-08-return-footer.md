# TUTORS ACADEMY — Phase 4 · Step 8
## Scene 8 — The Return, and the Footer

> Depends on Steps 4.1–4.7 (including the 4.4 availability amendment) and all of Phase 3.
> **Read the scene contract, the voice document, the honesty treatment, and Scenes 0–7's
> authored forms first.**
>
> **This step authors the final scene AND the page footer.** It does not touch the spine,
> Scenes 0–7, the subject system, or the primitives.
>
> **This closes the scene-authoring work of Phase 4.** Step 4.9 is the assembly, performance
> and accessibility pass over the whole page.

---

### WHY THIS STEP EXISTS

The visitor has been shown a system, offered doors, watched a crossing, read a credible account
of what's coming, and seen their own position on the path. This is the last scroll.

The scene has exactly one audience:

> **The visitor who read all of it and never crossed.**

Everyone else is already inside an environment and will never see this scene. **A scene built
for everyone is a scene built for no one** — so it is designed for the one person it exists for,
and it closes the page for everyone else.

---

### THE CORE IDEA — A RECALL, NOT A REPEAT

> **The page opens with a proposition and a door. It closes with the same proposition and the
> same door — and the same words mean something different now, because the visitor has earned
> the context to understand them.**

Scene 0 made a claim to someone who had seen nothing. Scene 8 makes **the same claim to someone
who has seen everything**: the system, the doors, the crossing, the honest account, and their own
position on the path.

- **This is circularity, not repetition** — a deliberate narrative device.
- **The restatement must land differently.** If it reads identically to the hero, it is lazy
  repetition and has failed.
- **The prompt requires you to justify the difference** — in the report, state precisely what the
  words now mean that they did not mean at Scene 0.

### THE SIX LOCKED DECISIONS

**1. The call to action is singuLAR — one action, not six.**

Scene 3 already offered six doors. **The visitor did not take one.** Re-presenting six options
repeats the exact decision that failed, at the point of lowest patience on the page.

- **Scene 8 offers ONE primary action, and it leads to the choice.**
- **This is a lower-friction door, not a second wall of doors.** It does not choose for the
  visitor — it removes the six-way decision from the final moment and returns them to the place
  where choosing is the point.
- **No featured, recommended, or default subject.** Scene 3's equal-weight rule holds; Scene 8
  must not smuggle in a favourite.
- **A secondary action is optional and must be justified.** If included, it moves *within* the
  page (back to a scene that answers a doubt) — never a second conversion path. **If you cannot
  justify it, omit it.**
- **Exactly one `primary` visible at this scroll position.** Report how the hierarchy resolves
  against Scene 4's CTA.

**2. The footer is PAGE CHROME, not scene content.**

- **The footer renders once, at the page level — outside the scene sequence — exactly like the
  nav shell.** **Architecture matters here:** if the footer lives inside a scene, reordering the
  spine changes the footer, and the spine is supposed to be freely reorderable.
- **It is not a scene.** It has no `narrativeFn`, no scroll behaviour, no scene contract entry.
  **Report where it lives structurally.**
- **It is brand frame** (2.6): the mark is brass, unchanged, subject-neutral. **No subject accent
  in the footer.**
- **It must not compete with the page's ending.** It follows Scene 8 and it is *quieter* than
  Scene 8 — chrome, not content.

**3. The footer is structurally honest — so it will be sparse, and sparse is correct.**

Most footers are a landfill: sitemaps of unbuilt pages, social icons for accounts that don't
exist, badges for apps that aren't published, accreditations nobody granted.

**Everything in this footer must exist.** Nothing decorative, nothing aspirational, nothing
placeholder-shaped.

**Banned in the footer, outright:**
- **Newsletter or email capture of any kind** — a form, an input, "subscribe", "get updates",
  "join the list". **Capture is banned on this page and stays banned here.**
- **Social media icons or handles** — there are no accounts. **A social icon is a claim.**
- **App store badges, "download the app", QR codes** — there is no app.
- **Accreditations, certifications, awards, memberships, "trusted by", partner logos.**
- **Student counts, ratings, statistics, testimonials — invented or otherwise.**
- **An address, phone number, or office location that does not exist.**
- **A sitemap of unbuilt pages, or links to routes that do not resolve.**
- **"Back to top" as decoration** — only if it is a real, keyboard-operable anchor that earns its
  place.

**Permitted:**
- The brand mark and wordmark (brand frame, unchanged).
- A short closing line in the 4.1 voice.
- **Links to real destinations only** — routes that resolve today.
- A copyright line using the brand name.
- **The honest legal status** (see Decision 4).

**4. Legal and contact are a LAUNCH BLOCKER, not Phase 10 polish.**

**Prerequisites for collecting a single student's data — report these as blockers, do not fake
them:**

- **Privacy policy** — required before any personal data is collected.
- **Terms of service** — required before anyone enrols.
- **A contact route** — how a student or parent reaches a human.
- **Data handling specifics for India** — the **DPDP Act (Digital Personal Data Protection Act,
  2023)** governs personal data of Indian users, including children's data, which is directly
  relevant to a student platform. **Flag this for legal review; do not attempt to draft policy
  copy.**
- **Cookies/analytics disclosure** if any analytics is added.

**Rules for this step:**
- **Do NOT create legal pages with placeholder or drafted legal copy.** Fake legal text is worse
  than absent legal text, because it looks like compliance.
- **Do NOT link to routes that do not exist.**
- **If a legal item is absent, the footer either omits it or states its status honestly** using
  the existing treatment vocabulary. **Prefer omission plus an explicit blocker report.**
- **Report the complete list of missing legal and contact items as launch blockers**, each with
  what it blocks. **This is a required deliverable and it matters more than the footer's
  appearance.**

**5. Scene 8 is brief. The page must END deliberately.**

- **Scene 8 is short** — it is a close, not a third peak. **Scene 7 was the peak; this is the
  door out.**
- **The page must end cleanly:** no awkward trailing whitespace, no orphaned scene, no footer
  beginning mid-viewport, no scroll that continues past the content into emptiness.
- **The last thing a visitor sees must be composed** — the footer should feel like the end of a
  building, not a dump of residue.
- **Copy budget for Scene 8:** lead ≤ 2 sentences, closing line 1 sentence, action label ≤ 4
  words. **Report actual counts.**

**6. The page is complete after this — so verify the whole journey reads.**

- **Read Scenes 0 → 8 in order, as a reader, and report honestly:**
  - Where does attention drop?
  - Which scene could be cut without loss?
  - Does the ending earn the beginning?
  - **Is the page too long?** Report the total word count and reading time, and give a plain
    verdict with a specific recommendation.
- **This is a required deliverable.** A nine-scene page assembled one scene at a time can be
  coherent in parts and exhausting as a whole. **Nobody has read it end to end yet.**

### BUILD — PART 1: SCENE 8 — THE COMPOSITION

**The job:** close the page, for the visitor who never crossed.

**Requirements:**
- **Brief and composed.** Quiet, decisive, not another spectacle. **Stillness again — Scene 7
  earned the emotional peak; Scene 8 is the exit.**
- **The recall of Scene 0's proposition** — same door, different meaning.
- **One primary action** (Decision 1).
- **No imagery, no illustration, no people, no scenic metaphor.**
- **No new visual language.** Type, the proximity principle, and at most one restrained structural
  element from the brand's language (3.3).
- **It must work with no motion.** **`reveal-once` at most**, or none at all — **report which.**
- **The composition must not duplicate Scene 7's.** Scene 7 was a still, centred emotional moment.
  **Scene 8 must read as a different kind of quiet** — an ending, not a peak. **Report the
  difference.**

**Mandatory deliverables — copy:**
- **Two candidates** for the recall — the returning statement. For each: **what these words mean
  now that they did not mean at Scene 0.** Verbatim, with a recommendation.
- **Two candidates** for the action label (≤ 4 words), checked against the 4.1 voice and against
  Scene 0's CTA language — **it must be coherent with the hero, not a different verb**.
- **The closing line** — 1 sentence, the page's last authored words before the footer.
- **The footer's closing line**, if it differs from the above.

### BUILD — PART 3: THE FOOTER

**Structure:**
- **`<footer>` landmark**, correctly labelled.
- **Rendered once at page level, outside the scene sequence** (Decision 2). **Report the
  structural location.**
- **Brand frame only** — the mark at an appropriate size, no subject accent.
- **Composition:** the mark, a closing line, real links, and the honest legal status. **That is
  the whole footer.** If it needs more to look complete, it is being padded — **report if you felt
  the pull to pad it.**
- **No columns of link groups for their own sake.** If there are three real links, there is one
  group of three, not three columns.
- **Its height must be proportionate to the page.** A nine-scene cinematic page does not end in a
  five-column mega-footer. **Report the footer's rendered height as a proportion of the page.**

**Content rules:**
- **Links only to routes that resolve today.** Verify each. **Report the list.**
- **No capture, no social, no badges, no accreditations, no stats, no address** (Decision 3's
  ban list). **Paste the sweep.**
- **Legal items:** omit or state honestly. **Do not link to non-existent routes. Do not draft
  legal copy.** Report the blockers (Decision 4).
- **Copyright line** using the brand name. **Report the exact string.**
- **All text uses the 2.2 type scale** — the footer is not a place for a new, smaller type ramp.

**Accessibility:**
- **Footer navigation carries a distinct accessible label** from the nav shell's and the story
  nav's (2.6's rule).
- **Ordered as a sensible reading sequence**, not DOM accident.
